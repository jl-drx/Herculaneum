## Independent synthetic mathematics

Twenty-five NumPy-only checks cover manual-only and supplied-support KL means, an explicitly weighted stratified illustration, teacher-to-student Bernoulli KL direction, stable extreme logits, ignored nonmanual target values, zero unsupported-output influence, derivative finite differences, sample-duplication invariance and unequal-support reductions. The initial illustrative stratified formula has its own declared coefficients and empty-extra policy; it is not the later root-selected allocation formula.

For a uniform supplied-support KL mean on G containing M, each manual KL derivative changes from lambda × (p−q)/|M| to lambda × (p−q)/|G|. Retaining lambda therefore preserves the aggregate coefficient but dilutes the manual per-output protection by |M|/|G|. An equal-batch risk also differs from a mean pooled over unequal pixel counts. These distinctions were verified before reviewing a scientific training result; none was run.

The root subsequently specified the narrower allocation comparison:

- A: mean BCE_M + 2 mean KL_M.
- B: mean BCE_M + mean KL_M + mean KL_retiredV.

Eighteen separate synthetic checks verify this exact comparison. Both strata are required to be nonempty and disjoint; an empty retired stratum stops both arms. Every retained scheduled batch meets these membership prerequisites. BCE remains restricted to manual targets, retired/unknown target values do not participate, and output derivatives are exactly zero outside M ∪ retired V.

The sums of KL group coefficients both equal two. B reallocates one unit from M to retired V, halves the manual KL derivative, and adds `(p−q)/|V|` on V. This tests preservation-budget allocation with other conditions held fixed. It does not preserve the old manual strength, and equal group weighting is not twice a uniform union mean when group sizes differ. Comparing different arms' own optimized objectives as a common training-risk measure would be invalid; both common formulas and individual terms should be retained. The reviewed root draft states these distinctions correctly and retains scientific interpretation and decision thresholds with the root.

The author's synthetic implementation and source were inspected separately. It retained an earlier synthetic-only STOP from requiring an exactly zero supported identity KL derivative. Its revised fixture allows 1e-15 FP64 roundoff; the observed split-objective identity derivative was 2.7755575615628914e-17. Exact zero KL values and unsupported derivatives remained required. This was not a scientific-result retry. The independent review's own float64 algebra/finite-difference bounds were unchanged and its checks passed on their first attempts. Any future first-step comparison between the real arms must have a separately frozen finite-precision contract; mathematical equality does not by itself promise bitwise-identical optimizer states.

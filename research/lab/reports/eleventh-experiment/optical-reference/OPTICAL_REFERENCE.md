# Legacy Frag4 optical-reference contract — 8 October 2026

**A concrete exposed-fragment IR/CT/ink-label bundle exists for PHercParis1Fr39. Its official small region descriptor links the rendered surface, mask and ink-label image. This closes asset discovery and declared canvas association; it does not yet establish optical registration accuracy, independently authored labels or a compatible model input.** No image, image header, CT chunk, label array or model prediction was read in this audit. This is a proposed reference route, not an executed eleventh model comparison.

## Physical identity and model exposure

The retained official catalog identifies **PHercParis1Fr39 / PHerc. Paris 1 Fr 39** as a fragment scanned at Diamond Light Source, and points explicitly to legacy **Frag4**. It says the fragment was withheld for automated scoring in the historical Kaggle competition and subsequently released. The catalog's current `scans`, `volumes` and `segments` maps are empty for this specimen, so a modern numeric scan/segment identity was not recovered. The complete retained record and its source hash are in [retained-catalog-extract.json](retained-catalog-extract.json).

This identity is distinct from **PHercParis4**, the scroll represented by `phercparis4-w00`, etc., in our cross-scroll models. Frag4 is also not the community's Scroll 4 / PHerc1667. The historical Kaggle designation does not establish independence from later models.

The actual checkpoint-configuration receipt in `reports/seventh-experiment/model-exposure-ledger.json` records 29 representations for each of seed42, seed43, Russo control75k and Russo ex01675k, all belonging to PHerc0139, PHerc1667, PHercParis4 or PHerc0814. None names PHercParis1Fr39, Paris1/Fr39 or Frag4. Our retained eighth–tenth adaptation and calibration records concern PHerc0139/PHerc0814, not this fragment. **Configured absence is established; upstream teacher, initialization/pretraining and historical pixel exposure remain unknown.** A new reservation can exclude all Frag4 aliases from our next updates and calibration, but cannot erase unresolved historical ancestry. This audit assigns no crop and does not reserve or evaluate an arm on the parent's behalf.

## What the small official descriptors prove

[legacy-surface-meta.json](legacy-surface-meta.json) describes the surface as **“PHercParis1Fr39 54KeV merged, segmented and converted with vc_layers to surface volume.”** [legacy-surface-render.json](legacy-surface-render.json), schema 0.1/type `region`, explicitly associates that surface folder with the same-basename surface-volume PPM, mask, inklabels, faux-RGB label and Volcart texture. It sets `invert_normals=false` and `bounding_box=null`. This is a useful official machine-readable association. It is not proof that a particular modern CT Zarr is in the PPM's coordinate frame, that the optical warp is accurate, or that the supplied mask represents all confidently negative pixels.

Two region exports name the same assets and give bounding boxes `[0,0,6064,4895]` and `[0,4895,6064,9790]`. The source metadata's height is 9791. We have not recovered endpoint semantics: treating these as half-open bounds would omit the final row, while inclusive bounds would overlap at 4895 and leave another issue at x=6064. Keep the original numbers; do not silently add one or use this split to define a holdout.

The 54 keV CT `.zattrs` uses Z/Y/X spatial axes and relative scale factors 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 across levels 0–5. **It supplies no µm unit or physical scale.** Nominal 3.24 µm is supported by the CT path name and the separately read surface metadata. Physical CT calibration and any merge/standardization transform between source and rendered coordinates still need a source receipt. A CT-native acquisition route is feasible in principle after that coordinate link is established; it is not ready simply because a chunk is small.

The source does not declare which of the 65 surface layers corresponds to the labeled optical face, the depth offset/step, sampling interpolation, local-normal orientation or historical `vc_layers` command/revision. `invert_normals=false` is a legacy descriptor flag, not proof of agreement with the released 9 µm model's normal orientation. Our verified modern [13,97), four-plane mean, 21-plane input/label-plane-10 recipe belongs to other 109-plane 2.399 µm renders and **cannot be applied to this 65-plane source**. Matching a desired plane count after a new resampling would not establish the missing physical depth relation. For example, factor-three averaging would nominally yield 9.72 µm if 3.24 µm really applies isotropically to the sampled surface, but no such crop/pooling/input convention has been authorized or validated here.

## Optical and annotation provenance still required

The retained official tutorial at Villa `e0bbb8b40a2db58b1d71864f286eb85717e59e64`, `scrollprize.org/docs/07_tutorial5.md`, lines 50–101, explains that detached fragments expose writing to 1000 nm IR and that the first fragment ink labels used IR aligned to surface volumes. That is method-level evidence. Its illustrated specimen is **PHerc. Paris 2 fr. 47**, not this fragment, so it does not authenticate Frag4's particular labels.

This bundle's original IR filename, candidate aligned IR, alignment PSD and linked ink-label PNG provide a concrete verification route. The inspected text does **not** identify the annotator, whether every label came from the IR rather than model assistance, the editable label source, the transformation/control points, registration residuals, mask meaning or supervision of negatives. No historical independent-test claim can be made yet. At first, any comparison against the PNG must be described as **agreement with the supplied fragment reference**, conditional on these limits. An accuracy claim needs independently supported optical annotation and measured registration/participation; visual stroke resemblance alone does not provide that.

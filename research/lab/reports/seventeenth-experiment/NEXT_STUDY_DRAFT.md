# Next study: depth-aware CT representation with preserved signal controls

This is a proposed research experiment, not a completed model or authorization to rent a GPU. Phase 17 found no clear local implementation/alignment fault explaining regional failure. Shared depth structure, variable surface metric and inconsistent class-intensity contrast motivate a representation hypothesis; none establishes its effectiveness.

Hypothesis: a model that can explicitly use surface-relative depth and changes across depth, while retaining the original intensity signal, will transfer more reliably across tissue texture than the unchanged seed42 representation. Keep the acquired depth support intact initially. Compare one frozen candidate against the unchanged baseline under matched training and inference budgets. Candidate inputs should retain raw CT, with a separately ablated depth coordinate/axial-difference branch; do not replace raw intensity with a tuned local subtraction. Local geometry may be a later separate ablation once its source-frame contract is explicit, rather than adding several unidentifiable changes at once.

Before training, freeze the exact architecture/adapter, initialization, training-only data, parent/alias exclusions, augmentation distribution, optimizer, step budget, operating-point calibration rule, cost ceiling and failure criteria. Restrict any physically motivated depth perturbation to training material and document its rationale before evaluation. Fit no shift, depth window, label warp or score inversion to improve Paris2. Use these six cases only as development diagnostics from now on; identify and quarantine fresh parent-level material before claiming confirmation. Preserve the calibration/evaluation distinction and account for upstream pretrained or teacher exposure.

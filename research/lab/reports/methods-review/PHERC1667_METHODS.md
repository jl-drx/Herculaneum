# What we inherit from the June 2026 PHerc.1667 result

Reviewed 8 October 2026. **We are applying the team's surface-conditioned ink-detection approach and using its public software, geometry and data. We have not reproduced its complete successful pipeline.** Our current detector is the later approximately 9 µm cross-scroll baseline, rather than a demonstrated copy of the detector used for the June reading.

The June 25 announcement concerns the surviving core of PHerc.1667: approximately 1.4 metres and the lower portions of around 22 columns. It does not imply that the missing parts of the original book were recovered. The methods preprint is *Complete virtual unwrapping and reading of a rolled Herculaneum papyrus*, arXiv:2606.29085v1, submitted June 27, 2026. This review uses the complete version-1 HTML, not only its abstract.

## The successful pipeline and our implementation

| Stage | June team's documented method | What our lab has actually done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | ESRF BM18 phase-contrast CT, approximately 2.4 µm isotropic voxels, 22 cm propagation distance, 78 keV average energy; a separate approximately 1.1 µm region improved some difficult readings. | Public PHerc0800 volume `20250521135224`: 8.640 µm, 1.2 m, 116 keV. Our PHerc1667 control originates from a 2.399 µm scan and was deliberately pooled to 9.596 µm for the later model. This is not native high-resolution June-model inference. |
| Surface reconstruction | Learned recto prediction followed by explicit TIFXYZ quad meshes, overlapping patch consensus, neighbouring-wrap transfer, manual corrections and local optimization. | We inherit public PHerc0800 meshes and pre-rendered surface volumes. We inspect mesh validity, preserve source coordinates and have reproduced 93 of 93 native CT depth samples at three local points. We have not traced, corrected, approved or unwrapped a new sheet. Sample agreement checks rendering provenance; they do not establish geometric correctness across a segment. |
| Flattening and sampling | Corrected quad mesh converted to triangles; SLIM with symmetric Dirichlet energy creates a low-distortion UV domain; rasterized surface maps back to CT. Rendering samples 65 positions along the surface normal, from −32 to +32 native voxels, using trilinear CT interpolation. | Public TIFXYZ coordinates and normal-oriented surface stacks are preserved. We have not rerun SLIM or measured flattening distortion. Our PHerc0800 source has 31 depth samples; its recorded exporter and depth order are retained. |
| Initial ink supervision | Infrared photographs of exposed fragments PHerc.9B, PHerc.343P and PHerc.500P2 aligned with CT surfaces; fragment-trained detector transfers initial signals to sealed scrolls. | We use released weights and annotations rather than aligning fragments or training a backbone. PHerc1667/w013 is a supplied-label control that overlaps our detector's stated training segment and representation. Its excellent annotation agreement is not held-out accuracy or independent proof of ink. |
| Ink architecture | 3D ResNet encoder with depth pooling and a 2D U-Net decoder. Documented ablation: ResNet3D-50 initialized from Kinetics-700, input `[62,256,256]`, approximately 0.614 mm XY at 2.4 µm; predictions initially quarter-resolution. | `scrollprize/ink_9um`, pinned August 14 revision: small local 3D stem and 2D U-Net, `[17,128,128]`, robust MAD normalization, approximately 9 µm isotropic inputs. At our target spacing the patch is 1.106 mm across. We reconstruct the model from checkpoint configuration, verify weights, retain raw scores and compare two seeds. These are materially different models and preprocessing recipes. |
| Adaptation and evaluation | Fragment backbone fine-tuned with conservative sealed-scroll pseudo-labels. Full reading used three columns for labeling and five iterations. A separate ablation used one labeling column and another unrolled column held out from labeling. | No PHerc0800 training, pseudo-label expansion or target-specific adaptation. We have depth shifts, reversal controls, seed comparisons and geometry-selected contexts; the latter are not a labeled target validation set. Two seeds trained on the same corpus are not independent physical validation. |
| Reading and translation | Eight qualified papyrologists examined renderings, predictions and geometric context. Accepted readings required correct surface placement, consistent letterforms across repeated renderings/inference, and independent endorsement or consensus. | Assistant visual review has found no coherent target letterforms. No human papyrologist review, target transcription or translation has been performed. Language models have not supplied letters or pseudo-labels. |

## Important distinctions

The paper identifies reproducibility code at villa commit [`e583fb67468f483fadd73d52e06f0ab0fe5ba813`](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/commit/e583fb67468f483fadd73d52e06f0ab0fe5ba813). Our focused unmodified source adapter uses later commit `e0bbb8b40a2db58b1d71864f286eb85717e59e64`. Both are official code, but their identity must not be collapsed.

The released [PHerc.1667 iteration 0–5 family](https://huggingface.co/collections/scrollprize/pherc1667-ink-detection-ablation) is explicitly the paper's `l_2` labeling / `l_5` held-out **ablation**, not proof that any one released checkpoint is the final three-column full-scroll detector. We retrieved all six public model manifests and the iteration-0/5 cards, configurations and model definitions; we did not download or run those weights. Iteration-0's card lists a cross-segment baseline and no `l_2` labels, so it should not be described as necessarily identical to the initial fragment-only backbone. Some card examples also differ in stated intensity handling; source preprocessing needs an explicit audit before running this family.

The [later official ink tutorial](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/blob/e0bbb8b40a2db58b1d71864f286eb85717e59e64/scrollprize.org/docs/07_tutorial5.md#ink-detection-at-9-µm-pretrained-cross-scroll-models) deliberately recommends our approximate-9-µm pretrained route for First Letters work. It documents depth-window sensitivity, both depth directions and checkpoint/seed comparisons. The [pinned model card](https://huggingface.co/scrollprize/ink_9um/blob/7109667e2607db1b90c37c8b09cb876ea7fe7bb1/README.md) confirms the four-scroll training corpus, jittered 17-of-21 planes and label smoothing. Our PHerc1667 control follows its published level-2 XY and four-plane depth mean-pooling, whereas the June detector operates on substantially finer native observations.

PHerc.Paris4's DINO-guided direct 3D ink segmentation is a separate high-resolution imaging-validation case in the paper. It supplies useful evidence that surface-conditioned output can correspond to physical deposits under favorable conditions. It is not a detector already shown to generalize to PHerc0800's 8.64 µm scan. Likewise, small patch context and excluding OCR reduce linguistic priors; neither removes ordinary material-texture false positives.

The team reported 31 wraps and 1,231 cm² unwrapped, with approximately 25 hours of manual annotation per wrap—roughly 775 hours if summed. That is a useful scale indication rather than a forecast for our target. Its full-scroll achievement required substantial geometry work and specialist interpretation, in addition to model inference. [Statistics and reproducibility](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29085v1#Sx4.SSx9.SSSx1).

## What this changes next

1. Continue a bounded, source-linked PHerc0800 search on larger valid interiors. Freeze selection independently of detector scores, preserve masks and check placement in CT before attributing a feature to ink. Larger displays provide character and line context; they do not add scan information.
2. Keep scan quality and exact prize eligibility separate. The refreshed [eligible-volume inventory](../../data/experiments/eligible-resolution-inventory/compatible-targets.json) matches all 22 current First Letters pairs to 8.640 or 9.362 µm volumes; only PHerc0800 lists released surface stacks for those exact eligible volumes. PHerc0800's public inventory currently lists the native 8.640 µm volume, with no verified higher-resolution eligible substitute. We cannot recreate 2.4 µm observations by enlarging this data. A bounded native-2.4-µm PHerc1667 reference experiment could more faithfully test the paper's method, but would remain a known-text control; exact final full-scroll weights, labels and preprocessing would still need proof. That replication is proposed, not accomplished.
3. Consider target adaptation only after a conservative, independently reviewed seed signal exists. Treat candidate predictions as hypotheses, keep separate physical regions out of labels and model selection, and record any pseudo-label lineage. Fine-tuning today's diffuse hotspot would risk reinforcing a texture response.
4. Before transcription, obtain human papyrological review satisfying the paper's placement, repeatability and independent-reading criteria. Our current null transcription/translation records remain appropriate.

These methods make a limited research attempt feasible. They do not justify promising recovery from every scan, a quick complete unwrapping, or a new-text award. The official [July 10 open-problems account](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/blob/e0bbb8b40a2db58b1d71864f286eb85717e59e64/scrollprize.org/docs/37_2026_open_problems.md) explicitly says pseudo-labeling can plateau or reveal no convincing ink, with the limiting stage varying by scroll.
